# CELL TYPE CHARACTERIZATION REPORT
## MERFISH Mouse Colon During Colitis Progression

---

## Executive Summary

This report provides an exhaustive characterization of **72 cell types** across **1,348,582 cells** in MERFISH spatial transcriptomics data from mouse colon during DSS-induced colitis. The analysis spans 4 disease stages (Healthy, DSS3, DSS9, DSS21) across 52 tissue slices, revealing dramatic cellular reorganization during inflammatory bowel disease progression.

**Key Findings:**
- **Immune cell expansion**: Massive infiltration of neutrophils, macrophages, B cells, and T cells during inflammation
- **Epithelial disruption**: Progressive loss of colonocytes and stem cells with emergence of inflammation-associated epithelial (IAE) populations
- **Stromal remodeling**: Expansion of inflammation-associated fibroblasts (IAF) and smooth muscle cells (IASMC)
- **Spatial reorganization**: Distinct cellular neighborhoods emerge during disease progression

---

## 1. Cell Type Composition Across Neighborhoods

### Overview
Analysis of normalized cell type fractions across 26 tissue neighborhoods reveals distinct spatial compartmentalization of immune, epithelial, and stromal populations.

### Key Patterns

**Immune-Enriched Neighborhoods:**
- Neighborhoods show strong enrichment for inflammation-associated fibroblasts (IAF 1-5), macrophages, and T cells
- These neighborhoods likely represent organized immune niches in inflamed regions
- B cells and plasma cells cluster in specific neighborhoods, suggesting tertiary lymphoid structure formation

**Epithelial-Dominated Neighborhoods:**
- Early neighborhoods are dominated by colonocytes, goblet cells, and enteroendocrine cells
- Minimal immune infiltration in these regions indicates intact mucosal architecture
- Stem cells and transit-amplifying (TA) cells localize to specific crypt-associated neighborhoods

**Stromal/Mesenchymal Compartments:**
- Mid-range neighborhoods concentrate smooth muscle cells (SMC), fibroblasts, glia, and neurons
- These define the muscularis and submucosal compartments
- Scattered inflammation-associated populations indicate stromal remodeling during disease

### Biological Interpretation
The clear compartmentalization suggests that colitis induces organized spatial reorganization rather than uniform inflammation. Immune cells cluster in specific neighborhoods, potentially representing sites of active tissue damage or repair. The preservation of epithelial-dominated neighborhoods indicates regional heterogeneity in disease severity.

## 2. Cell Type Dynamics Across Disease Stages

### Inflammation-Associated Cell Types

**Neutrophils (Neutrophil 1, 2):**
- Nearly absent in healthy tissue
- Massive infiltration at DSS3 (acute inflammation)
- Persist through DSS9 and DSS21
- Spatial pattern: Widespread mucosal infiltration
- **Role**: Early innate immune response to epithelial damage

**Inflammation-Associated Epithelium (IAE 1, 2, 3):**
- Sparse in healthy tissue
- Dramatic expansion at DSS3/DSS9
- Reduction during recovery (DSS21)
- Spatial pattern: Epithelial layer/crypt structures
- **Role**: Stress-responsive epithelial population during acute inflammation

**Inflammation-Associated Fibroblasts (IAF 1-5):**
- Virtually absent in healthy tissue
- Progressive accumulation through DSS3 → DSS9 → DSS21
- Spatial pattern: Epithelial-adjacent, stromal compartments
- **Role**: Tissue remodeling, fibrosis, and inflammatory signaling

**B Cells and Plasma Cells:**
- Sparse in healthy tissue
- Dramatic expansion at DSS3, peak at DSS9
- Persist at DSS21
- Spatial pattern: Organized lymphoid aggregates
- **Role**: Adaptive immune response, antibody production

### Epithelial Cell Types

**Colonocytes:**
- Abundant in healthy tissue forming organized crypt lining
- Progressive depletion through DSS3/DSS9
- Partial recovery at DSS21
- **Role**: Epithelial barrier maintenance

**Goblet Cells (Goblet 1, 2):**
- Maintain epithelial positioning throughout disease
- Variable abundance across stages
- **Role**: Mucus secretion and barrier function

**Stem Cells and TA Cells:**
- Severely reduced or absent in DSS-treated samples
- Heterogeneous recovery patterns
- **Role**: Epithelial regeneration

### Stromal Cell Types

**Smooth Muscle Cells (SMC 1, 2, IASMC 1-3):**
- Maintain peripheral localization throughout disease
- IASMC subtypes emerge during inflammation
- **Role**: Structural support and contractility

**Fibroblasts (Fibro 1-15):**
- Multiple subtypes with distinct spatial patterns
- Disease-dependent expansion of specific subtypes
- **Role**: ECM production, tissue remodeling

### Immune Cell Types

**Macrophages (Cxcl10+, Itgax+, Lyve1+, Mrc1+):**
- Multiple functional subtypes
- Spatial clustering in inflammatory foci
- **Role**: Phagocytosis, antigen presentation, tissue remodeling

**T Cells (CD4+, CD8+, Tregs, Tfh):**
- Progressive infiltration during disease
- Spatial organization in lymphoid aggregates
- **Role**: Adaptive immunity and immune regulation

## 3. Spatial Distribution Patterns

### Cell Type-Specific Localization

**Epithelial Layer:**
- Colonocytes, Goblet cells, EEC, M cells
- IAE populations during inflammation
- Stem cells and TA cells in crypts

**Lamina Propria/Submucosa:**
- Immune cells (macrophages, T cells, B cells)
- Fibroblasts and IAF populations
- Dendritic cells and plasma cells

**Muscularis:**
- Smooth muscle cells (SMC 1, 2)
- IASMC populations during inflammation
- Enteric neurons and glia

**Vasculature:**
- Endothelial cells (arterial, venous, capillary, lymphatic)
- Pericytes

### Disease-Induced Spatial Reorganization

**Healthy → DSS3 (Acute Inflammation):**
- Massive neutrophil infiltration
- Emergence of IAE and IAF populations
- B cell and T cell recruitment
- Epithelial barrier disruption

**DSS3 → DSS9 (Peak Inflammation):**
- Continued immune cell expansion
- Further epithelial loss
- Stromal remodeling
- Formation of organized lymphoid structures

**DSS9 → DSS21 (Chronic/Recovery):**
- Persistent immune populations
- Partial epithelial recovery
- Fibrotic remodeling (IAF expansion)
- Establishment of new tissue architecture

## 5. Biological Insights

### Inflammation Cascade
1. **Initiation**: Epithelial damage triggers neutrophil recruitment
2. **Amplification**: Macrophages and dendritic cells amplify inflammatory signals
3. **Adaptive Response**: B and T cell infiltration, lymphoid structure formation
4. **Chronicity**: Persistent immune activation, fibrotic remodeling

### Tissue Remodeling
- Emergence of inflammation-associated cell states (IAE, IAF, IASMC)
- Loss of homeostatic epithelial populations
- Stromal expansion and fibrosis
- Vascular remodeling

### Spatial Organization
- Formation of immune-enriched neighborhoods
- Preservation of some epithelial compartments
- Establishment of new tissue architecture in chronic disease

---

## 6. Conclusions

This comprehensive spatial analysis reveals:

1. **Cellular Heterogeneity**: 72 distinct cell types with unique spatial distributions
2. **Dynamic Reorganization**: Dramatic changes in cellular composition across disease stages
3. **Spatial Compartmentalization**: Organized neighborhoods with specialized functions
4. **Inflammation-Associated States**: Emergence of novel cell states during disease
5. **Incomplete Recovery**: Persistent alterations in chronic disease despite treatment cessation

The data provides a detailed spatial atlas of colitis progression, highlighting potential therapeutic targets and biomarkers for inflammatory bowel disease.
